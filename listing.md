# App Store listing copy

Working copy for the Shopify App Store submission. Not part of the merchant
docs nav — it's here so it lives with the rest of the writing.

Every claim below is checked against what the app actually does as of
2026-09-25. **If a feature changes, change this too** — App Store requirement
4.3 is about listing accuracy, and 1.1.4 prohibits unfactual claims.

---

## App name

**Easy Quotes**

Under 30 characters, brand-led, no competitor or platform name. Shopify
requires the listing name and the name in `shopify.app.toml` to match or be
clearly similar — both are "Easy Quotes".

## App introduction (100 characters max)

> Let shoppers request a quote instead of buying. Each request becomes a draft
> order you can price.

98 characters. States the benefit, avoids superlatives, no statistics — all
three are explicit listing requirements.

## App details

> Some products don't have one price. Made-to-order goods, wholesale and trade
> accounts, freight-heavy items, installations — the number depends on the job,
> so the sale starts with a conversation.
>
> Easy Quotes gives those stores a second path through the storefront. Shoppers
> collect products into a quote request instead of a cart, answer the questions
> you need answered, and send it. You get a draft order in Shopify with their
> answers attached, ready to price and invoice.
>
> Nothing leaves Shopify. You set the prices, send the invoice, and the customer
> pays through your own checkout with your own shipping and tax rules.
>
> **Design the form you actually need.** Text, paragraph, email, phone, number
> and dropdown fields, grouped into sections. Relabel or switch off any of the
> built-in contact and address fields. On Pro, add radio buttons and checkboxes,
> and show a field only when an earlier answer calls for it.
>
> **Put the button where it belongs.** Add to quote goes on product pages
> through the theme editor, and on themes built with theme blocks it works
> inside product cards too, so it appears in every collection grid. Older themes
> get a snippet.
>
> **Decide who sees it.** Everyone, logged-in customers only, customers with a
> tag you control, or B2B customers. Hide prices and Add to cart on the products
> that need a conversation first — and never on the ones that don't.
>
> **Know what it's worth.** The dashboard shows quotes over time, how many were
> paid, and what's still open.
>
> Quote requests are emailed to you the moment they arrive, and replies go
> straight to the shopper.

No statistics, no "best"/"first"/"only", no guarantees. Each paragraph names a
capability a reviewer can verify in the app.

## Feature list (up to 5, short)

1. Add to quote button on product pages and in collection grids
2. Drag-and-drop quote form with sections and conditional fields
3. Every request becomes a Shopify draft order with the answers attached
4. Hide prices and Add to cart for trade-only or quote-only products
5. Quote dashboard, email notifications, webhooks and Klaviyo

## Search terms

Reviewers don't rank these, but in-store search is where most installs come
from. Cover the language merchants actually use:

`request a quote`, `quote form`, `request for quote`, `RFQ`, `hide price`,
`hide add to cart`, `call for price`, `B2B quote`, `wholesale pricing`,
`trade pricing`, `custom quote`, `draft order`, `quote builder`

## Pricing details

| Plan | Price | Trial |

Say plainly that Free does not accept new quote requests — an unstated
limitation on a free tier is the kind of thing review catches.

## Demo store

Use a store with a real catalogue where the flow makes obvious sense. Link
directly to a product page with the Add to quote button on it, not the home
page, and add instructions: *"Add this to a quote, then open the quote link in
the header to submit the form."*

# Screenshot shot list

**6 images, 1600 × 900 (16:9), PNG.** Crop out browser chrome. No pricing
figures, no statistics, no review requests — all three are prohibited in
listing images. Real data from a real catalogue; blur or replace any customer
name or email.

Order matters: the first two are what most merchants look at.

### 1. Add to quote on a product page
The storefront product page with the Add to quote button next to Add to cart.
The most-asked question a merchant has is "what does my customer see" — answer
it first.
**Caption:** Shoppers request a quote without leaving your product page.

### 2. The quote page with items and the form
Your quote page showing two or three items alongside the form, mid-scroll so
both are visible.
**Caption:** They review their items and answer your questions in one place.

### 3. The draft order in Shopify admin
Shopify's own draft order screen for a submitted quote, with the line items and
the quote answers visible in the attributes.
**Caption:** Every request lands in Shopify as a draft order, answers attached.

### 4. The form builder
The Easy Quotes form builder with a form that has sections and a few field
types — enough to show it's a real builder, not a fixed form.
**Caption:** Build the form you need — sections, field types, conditional
questions.

### 5. The dashboard
The app home page with the chart and the metric tiles, on a store with enough
history that the chart isn't empty. **Do not show revenue figures** if they'd
read as a performance claim; a store with modest numbers is safer than one that
looks like a testimonial.
**Caption:** See how many quotes turn into paid orders.

### 6. Collection grid buttons
A collection page with Add to quote under several product cards.
**Caption:** Works in collection grids too, not just product pages.

## Feature video (optional, strongly recommended)

2–3 minutes, promotional rather than instructional, screencast under 25% of
the runtime. The strongest version is the shopper's journey end to end —
product page, quote page, submit — then a two-second cut to the draft order
appearing in Shopify. That single cut is the whole product.
